## Introduction

Albert Einstein reportedly called compound interest **"the eighth wonder of the world."**  
He said: *"He who understands it, earns it. He who doesn't, pays it."*

In simple words — when you invest ₹10,000 today at 7% interest, next year you don't just earn on ₹10,000… you earn on ₹10,700. That small extra interest starts earning interest too. Over 10–20 years, this snowball effect turns modest savings into serious wealth.

This guide will show you exactly how it works in the Indian context — with formulas, calculators, real bank rates in 2025, and practical examples.

## The Compound Interest Formula (Made Super Easy)

**A = P (1 + r/n)^(n×t)**

Where:
- **A** = Final amount
- **P** = Principal (your initial investment)
- **r** = Annual interest rate (in decimal)
- **n** = Number of times interest is compounded per year
- **t** = Time in years

### Indian Bank FD Reality (2025)
Most banks compound **quarterly** (n = 4)

**Quick Formula for Indian FDs:**
**A = P (1 + r/4)^(4×t)**

### Real Example (2025 Rates)
You invest ₹5 lakh in an FD at **7.5% p.a.** for **5 years** (quarterly compounding)

**A = 5,00,000 × (1 + 0.075/4)^(4×5) = ₹7,13,340**

You earn ₹2,13,340 interest — almost 43% return in 5 years!

## Rule of 72 – How to Estimate Doubling Time

Want to know how long it takes to double your money?

**Years to double ≈ 72 ÷ interest rate**

## Recurring Deposit (RD) vs Lump Sum FD – Which is Better?

| Parameter       | Lump Sum FD            | Recurring Deposit (RD)      |
|-----------------|------------------------|-----------------------------|
| Best for        | One-time surplus       | Monthly savings habit       |
| Interest earned | Higher (full amount compounds) | Lower (each installment compounds for less time) |
| Example (₹5k/month, 8%, 5 yrs) | —                  | ~₹3.75 lakh total           |
| Lump sum ₹3 lakh @8% 5 yrs     | ~₹4.41 lakh        | —                           |

**Winner:** If you already have the money — go for lump sum FD or SIP in debt funds.
